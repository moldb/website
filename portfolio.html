Viral genomics · AI models · In progress

HIV-1 promoter (LTR) variants analyzed with AlphaGenome

Question

Naturally occurring single-nucleotide variants in the HIV-1 long terminal repeat (LTR), the viral promoter, were linked in our earlier cohort studies to disease severity and to neurocognitive impairment. Can a state-of-the-art sequence-to-function AI model predict the regulatory effects of those same variants?

Approach

Using Google DeepMind’s AlphaGenome model to score LTR sequence variants and interpreting the predictions against the published experimental and clinical findings, with attention to where the model’s training data and assumptions do and do not fit a viral promoter.

What it shows

A working example of the AI validation I offer clients: a modern model applied to a question where I already know the biology, so its output can be judged against evidence rather than taken on faith.

Background evidence

  • HIV-1 promoter SNPs associated with clinical disease severity PLoS One
  • Co-selected promoter SNPs precede neurocognitive impairment J. NeuroVirology

Expert evaluation of AI in scientific research

Question

AI tools produce fluent, confident scientific answers. Which of those answers would survive review by an experienced scientist, and how can an organization test that systematically before relying on them?

Approach

Testing AI systems on questions whose answers I know from my own research, and building structured evaluation tasks from real analyses so that model output can be scored against expert-verified results rather than plausibility.

What it shows

A practical method for separating reliable AI output from confident error, the core of my AI Scientific Validation service.

Drug discovery · Cheminformatics · NIH-funded

Chemalytics: cloud platform for virtual screening and small-molecule hit prediction

Question

Can the likely interactions and function of a small molecule be predicted computationally, quickly enough to rank very large compound libraries before any wet-lab screening?

Approach

Developed ChemVassa, a method for identifying small-molecule hits, and built it into a cloud computing platform for ultra-high-throughput prediction, docking and ranking. I led the science, the software architecture and the grant strategy, and applied the platform with pharmaceutical and academic partners in cancer, HIV and antimicrobial programs.

Outcome

Peer-reviewed method publication, two patents including novel LYN kinase inhibitors, and NIH SBIR funding from Phase I feasibility through Phase II development.

HIV genetics · Clinical cohort · Sequence analysis

HIV-1 genetic variation and disease outcomes in a patient cohort

Question

Do naturally occurring changes in the HIV-1 genome, including in the viral promoter (LTR) and in the Tat, Vpr and envelope-coreceptor regions, help explain why disease severity, viral persistence and neurocognitive outcomes differ so widely between patients?

Approach

Sequence analysis of viral genes from patients followed longitudinally in a clinical HIV genetic-analysis cohort, in collaboration with virologists and clinicians. The work related LTR sequence variants, and genetic signatures in the LTR, Tat and Vpr, to clinical disease measures, coreceptor usage, substance use, aging, and sampling from blood versus brain.

Outcome

LTR variants associated with clinical disease severity, co-selected promoter variants that precede the onset of neurocognitive impairment, and distinct genetic signatures separating CXCR4- and CCR5-using virus. These findings are the experimental and clinical baseline against which I now interpret the AlphaGenome analysis above.

Evidence

  • HIV-1 promoter SNPs associated with clinical disease severity PLoS One
  • Co-selected promoter SNPs precede neurocognitive impairment J. NeuroVirology
  • Differential genetic signatures in CXCR4- and CCR5-utilizing HIV-1 PLoS One

Biomarkers · Immunology · Clinical collaboration

MicroRNA biomarkers in chronic obstructive pulmonary disease

Question

Why do some smokers develop COPD while others do not, and can microRNA expression in blood and immune cells point to markers of disease and to potential points of intervention?

Approach

Analysis of microRNA expression profiles from patients with COPD and from unaffected smokers, in collaboration with pulmonary and immunology researchers at Temple University.

Outcome

Identified a microRNA signature distinguishing smokers with and without COPD, and reduced expression of miR-199a-5p in the regulatory T cells of patients with COPD.

Evidence

  • Blunted expression of miR-199a-5p in regulatory T cells of patients with COPD Clin. Exp. Immunol.
  • MicroRNA signature in the peripheral blood of smokers and patients with COPD ATS abstract

Pharmaceutical R&D · Genomics

Genomics for target discovery in pharmaceutical research

Question

How can early genomic and gene-expression technologies be turned into reliable information about drug targets, including the gene variants and splice forms that change how a target behaves?

Approach

As head of bioinformatics at Warner-Lambert, I built the computational biology capability behind discovery research: sequence analysis for target identification and methods for extracting biological signal from high-throughput DNA microarray data.

Outcome

A method for predicting alternative splice variants from DNA chip expression data, published in Genome Research, and a patent on the gene for the α2δ calcium-channel subunit.

Proteomics · Bioinformatics

Computational foundations for high-throughput proteome analysis

Question

Before large-scale proteomics was routine, could proteins from an organism with a small genome be identified uniquely and at high throughput from mass and gel data alone?

Approach

Computational analysis of the predicted E. coli proteome combined with experimental gel electrophoresis and mass spectrometry data, in collaboration with protein chemists and microbiologists.

Outcome

A theoretical demonstration that high-throughput proteome analysis was feasible, followed by a “virtual 2-D gel” approach to visualizing the E. coli proteome by mass spectrometry.

Evidence

  • Unique identification of proteins from small genome organisms Electrophoresis
  • Escherichia coli proteome analysis using the gene-protein database Electrophoresis
  • Virtual 2-D gel electrophoresis of the E. coli proteome Analytical Chemistry
